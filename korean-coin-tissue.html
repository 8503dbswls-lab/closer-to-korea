What Is the Coin-Shaped Tissue You May Encounter at Restaurants the Korea-Based Editor Visits?

A small, dry disc can become a usable tissue after you add water. Here is what Korean coin tissue is and where Korean product listings say it can be used.

Published September 2, 2026Updated October 14, 2026

AI-generated illustration for explanatory purposes.

What Is Korean Coin Tissue?

Coin tissue, called 동전티슈 in Korean, is a small, dry compressed tissue that expands when water is added. The Korean name describes the coin-like shape; it does not identify a particular brand or country of manufacture.

How Does It Work?

Add water to the compressed disc and let it expand, then gently unfold it before use. These are the steps described by manufacturer HUASHENG for its coin-tissue product; follow the instructions on the product you receive.

Where Might You Use It?

CleanZone describes restaurant and outdoor uses for its coin tissue. HUASHENG lists restaurants, travel, camping, and home use for its product. These are manufacturers’ suggested uses, not evidence that every Korean restaurant provides coin tissue.

Is It Common at Korean Restaurants?

Not necessarily. In the Korea-based editor's local observation, coin tissue is not something commonly provided everywhere, although diners may encounter it at some restaurants.

Does “Korean Coin Tissue” Mean It Was Made in Korea?

No. A Korean name or sale in Korea does not establish where an item was made. HUASHENG is a manufacturer in China that lists Korea among its export markets. Check the packaging or the exact manufacturer’s information for a specific product’s origin. The sources below explain the product type; they do not identify a brand or model in the illustration.

Final Takeaway

If a restaurant provides a coin tissue, it is used after adding water and unfolding it. Availability varies, and the product’s packaging is the best starting point for checking its instructions and origin.

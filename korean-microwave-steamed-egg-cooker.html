What Is a Korean Microwave Steamed Egg Cooker?

Learn what a Korean microwave steamed egg cooker is, how it is used for gyeran-jjim, and how it differs from a traditional Korean ttukbaegi.

Published July 30, 2026Updated August 17, 2026

A white ceramic microwave steamed egg cooker with a lid, side handles, and ribbed sides.

You may have seen a small ceramic pot with a lid in a Korean kitchen without knowing what it was called.

This type of container is best described in English as a Korean microwave steamed egg cooker or microwave gyeran-jjim cooker. It is used to make gyeran-jjim, a soft Korean steamed egg dish, in a microwave.

The cooker shown in this article is a ceramic container that was sold through Daiso Korea. It has a lid, two side handles, and a small steam hole. It looks a little like a traditional Korean pot, but it is designed for microwave use rather than direct heat.

Quick Answer

A Korean microwave steamed egg cooker is a small microwave-safe container used to prepare gyeran-jjim more conveniently.

It is not the same as a traditional black ttukbaegi, which may be used over direct heat depending on the product. The bottom of this specific cooker states that it is microwave- and dishwasher-safe and should not be used over direct flame.

What Is It Called?

The Korean product name is 전자레인지용 계란찜기.

A natural English translation is microwave steamed egg cooker.

  • Korean microwave egg cooker
  • Microwave gyeran-jjim cooker
  • Ceramic microwave egg steamer
  • Korean steamed egg bowl with lid
  • Ceramic microwave bowl for steamed eggs

The Korean word gyeran-jjim means steamed eggs. The word jjim-gi can refer to a cooker or container used to prepare a steamed dish.

There is no single standard English name for this type of product, so American shoppers may search by its appearance or purpose instead of its Korean name.

However, appearance alone does not tell you whether a ceramic container is safe for a microwave, dishwasher, oven, or direct heat. The markings on the bottom should always be checked.

How It Is Used

The main purpose of this cooker is to prepare gyeran-jjim in a microwave.

Eggs are mixed with seasonings and optional ingredients, poured into the container, covered, and cooked in the microwave. The exact amount of egg, added liquid, cooking time, and microwave setting will depend on the recipe and the appliance.

Why Koreans Eat Gyeran-Jjim

Gyeran-jjim is a familiar Korean egg dish with a soft texture. It may be served as a side dish, as part of a home meal, or as a simple dish on its own.

Traditional versions may be cooked in a ttukbaegi or another pot over heat. In modern homes, people may also use a microwave-safe bowl or a dedicated microwave cooker when they want a faster and simpler option.

This product represents a practical side of everyday life in Korea: a familiar dish prepared with a small, convenient kitchen tool.

It is not an ancient or ceremonial object. It is a modern household item connected to a traditional-style Korean dish.

Is It a Ttukbaegi?

Not exactly.

A ttukbaegi is a Korean earthenware pot often used for soups, stews, and steamed eggs. Many ttukbaegi products are designed to hold heat well, and some can be used over direct heat.

This microwave egg cooker may resemble a small ttukbaegi, but the bottom marking specifically says:

  • Microwave & Dishwasher Safe
  • Do Not Use Over Direct Flame
  • Ceramic Tableware
  • Made in China

For that reason, it should not be placed on a gas burner or used over an open flame.

Do You Need a Special Cooker for Gyeran-Jjim?

No.

You do not need a dedicated cooker to make gyeran-jjim. A suitable microwave-safe bowl may also work.

A dedicated cooker may simply make the process more convenient because it has:

  • A lid

  • A compact size

  • A steam hole

The result will still depend on the recipe, cooking time, microwave wattage, and container size.

A special cooker cannot guarantee the same texture in every microwave.

What to Look for in a Similar Product

Microwave Safety

The listing or product label should clearly say that the container is microwave-safe.

Do not assume that every ceramic bowl can be used in a microwave.

Lid Design

Check whether the lid has a steam hole or instructions explaining how steam should escape.

A tightly sealed lid may not be appropriate for microwave cooking.

Material

Confirm whether the product is made from ceramic, stoneware, glass, or another microwave-safe material.

Capacity

The container should have enough space for the egg mixture to expand during cooking.

Handles

Handles may make the container easier to move, but they can still become hot.

Dishwasher Safety

Microwave-safe and dishwasher-safe are separate features. Check both.

Direct Heat Warning

Do not use a microwave cooker on a stovetop unless the manufacturer specifically allows it.

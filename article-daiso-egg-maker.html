This Tiny Daiso Gadget Makes Korean-Style Steamed Eggs in Minutes

A microwave shortcut for gyeran-jjim sounds like a perfect small-kitchen win. Here is what it appears designed to do, why the dish matters, and what must be verified before use.

By Closer to Korea EditorsJuly 28, 20268-minute read

Gyeran-jjim (계란찜) is a savory Korean steamed egg dish made by cooking beaten eggs with water or broth until soft and custardy. It is familiar across home cooking and restaurant meals, though texture, seasoning, and add-ins vary.

How it is used

The broad method is to beat egg with water or broth, season it, place it in a microwave-safe vessel, and cook in short intervals until just set. The product’s verified instructions must take priority because wattage, vessel size, fill level, venting, and material affect safe use.

  2. Whisk the egg mixture separately.
  3. Do not exceed any verified fill line.
  4. Use only the time and wattage range stated by the manufacturer.
  5. Let the container rest before opening; escaping steam can burn.

Why steamed egg feels familiar in Korea

Steamed egg is a widely recognized Korean dish with a soft texture and savory seasoning. It can appear as banchan, beside grilled food, or in a simple home meal. “Everyday” does not mean every household serves the same version; it means the dish is broadly familiar and adaptable.

Why a microwave version is convenient

The use case is practical: one or two servings, limited time, and minimal cleanup. It lowers the friction of making a gentle egg side dish without claiming to replace every traditional method.

Cleaning and safety

Rinse after the vessel cools and wash according to package directions. Do not assume dishwasher safety. Inspect the lid, vent, hinges, and surface for damage. Stop use if it warps, cracks, develops deep scratches, or no longer closes as intended.

Use oven mitts or a dry towel, keep your face away from the opening, and direct steam away from you. Children should use microwave cookware only with appropriate adult supervision.

Why Koreans use it

The appeal is practical: eggs are flexible, the dish is comforting, and a microwave can make a small portion easier on a busy day. A verified microwave-safe bowl may also work, so the dedicated tool earns its place only if its shape, venting, portioning, or cleanup is meaningfully better.

What Americans should know

Do not expect scrambled eggs, an omelet, or a sweet custard. Gyeran-jjim is savory, moist, and spoonable. Texture ranges from silky to fluffy, and there is no single universal recipe.

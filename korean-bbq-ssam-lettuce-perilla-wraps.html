What Is Ssam? Korean BBQ Lettuce and Perilla Leaf Wraps

At Korean barbecue, diners may wrap grilled meat, rice, sauces, and side dishes in leafy greens. This eating style is called ssam, and the combination can be assembled according to personal taste.

Published 2026-09-13

What does ssam mean?

Ssam is a Korean food culture in which leafy vegetables are used to wrap meat, rice, seasoning, or side dishes. At a Korean barbecue restaurant, the leaf becomes a small wrapper for ingredients placed inside it.

Lettuce and perilla leaves

Lettuce and perilla leaves are introduced as representative ssam ingredients in Korean barbecue. Diners can choose one leaf, combine them, or adjust the filling to suit their preferences.

What is a perilla leaf?

The Korean word kkaennip refers to a perilla leaf, also called a deulkkae leaf—not a sesame leaf. Perilla and sesame are different plants, despite occasional confusion in English.

How diners assemble a ssam

A diner might place grilled meat on a leaf and add rice, sauce, or a side dish before folding it. The combination is a matter of personal preference. From a Korea-based local-observation perspective, some diners may prefer to eat a prepared ssam in one bite, but this is not a universal rule.

Is ssam only found in Korea?

No. Ssam and perilla have also been introduced in the United States through Korean barbecue restaurants and food media, so this eating style is not limited to Korea. English-language community discussions also include questions about the leafy vegetables served with Korean barbecue, but they should not be treated as representative of all Americans.

Final Takeaway

Ssam is best understood as a flexible way to build a bite with leafy greens, meat, rice, sauce, and side dishes. At Korean barbecue, lettuce and perilla are two useful starting points, but the final wrap can vary with the diner’s preferences.

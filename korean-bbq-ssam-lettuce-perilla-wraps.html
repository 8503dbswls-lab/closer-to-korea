What Is Ssam? Korean BBQ Lettuce and Perilla Leaf Wraps

The leaves served with Korean barbecue are often lettuce (sangchu) and perilla (kkaennip). They hold grilled meat, rice, garlic, ssamjang, or other fillings. Ssam is the Korean way of wrapping those ingredients into a bite, with room for your own preferences.

Published 2026-09-13 · Updated 2026-10-16

What does ssam mean?

Ssam (쌈) means a wrap and describes a way of eating: place a small amount of food in a leaf or another wrapper, fold it around the filling, and eat it. Lettuce and perilla are familiar choices at Korean barbecue, but the wrapping method can also use other ingredients.

Lettuce and perilla leaves

Lettuce is called sangchu (상추) in Korean. Its shape and color vary by variety. Loose-leaf lettuce often has broad, wavy or ruffled leaves, which may be green or reddish. Perilla leaves, called kkaennip (깻잎), are generally broad and rounded to oval, with a pointed tip and a noticeably toothed edge. Their distinct aroma is another useful clue.

You can use either leaf on its own or layer lettuce and perilla together before adding the filling.

From our Korea-based editor: "To me, lettuce has a light taste. Perilla has its own aroma and a mild, clean taste that I enjoy, so I prefer wrapping my food in perilla leaves." This is a personal taste preference; another diner may enjoy the leaves differently.

What is a perilla leaf?

The Korean word kkaennip refers to a perilla leaf, also called a deulkkae leaf. Perilla and sesame are different plants, despite the occasional use of "sesame leaf" for kkaennip in English.

Perilla leaves have fine hairs on the underside. Our editor notes that this surface can feel slightly rough in the mouth to someone sensitive to texture: "I sometimes turn the leaf over when making my ssam." Reversing the leaf is a personal way to adjust the texture, not a rule for eating it.

How diners assemble a ssam

Place a piece of grilled meat on a leaf, then add the fillings you like. Garlic and ssamjang are very common companions in the Korean meals our editor describes. Ssamjang is a seasoned dipping paste commonly made with fermented soybean paste and red chili paste. A little rice or a side dish can go into the wrap too.

Garlic and ssamjang are familiar choices, not mandatory ingredients. Adjust the amounts or leave something out to suit your taste. Some diners prefer to eat a prepared ssam in one bite, but this is not a universal rule.

Ssammu: thin pickled radish wraps

Ssammu (쌈무), also written ssam-mu, is radish sliced into thin sheets and pickled for wrapping food. You can wrap meat or other fillings in a sheet without lettuce or perilla, or layer it with the leaves in the same ssam.

Our editor has also seen light green and purplish versions in Korea. The color alone does not tell you which ingredients were used, so check the particular product if that matters to you.

Onion cups are another option

Onion ssam uses a separated, curved layer of onion like a little cup. Put the fillings inside it instead of wrapping them in a leaf. Our editor remembers onion ssam drawing attention in Korea, and a 2019 Nongmin report describes the idea.

Is ssam only found in Korea?

No. Ssam and perilla have also been introduced in the United States through Korean barbecue restaurants and food media, so this eating style is not limited to Korea. English-language community discussions also include questions about the leafy vegetables served with Korean barbecue, but they should not be treated as representative of all Americans.

Final Takeaway

Start with lettuce, perilla, or both, then choose the fillings and seasonings you enjoy. Ssammu and onion cups offer other ways to hold a bite. The leaf, the filling, and even which side of a perilla leaf faces out can reflect personal preference.

Frequently asked questions

What is ssam in Korean food?
Ssam is a Korean food culture in which leafy vegetables wrap ingredients such as meat, rice, seasoning, or side dishes.
What leaves are used for Korean BBQ ssam?
Lettuce and perilla leaves are representative ssam ingredients introduced with Korean barbecue. The exact combination can vary by personal preference.
Is kkaennip a sesame leaf?
No. Kkaennip is a perilla or deulkkae leaf, and perilla is a different plant from sesame.
Do you have to eat ssam in one bite?
No universal rule requires that. From a Korea-based local-observation perspective, some diners may prefer one bite, but eating preferences can differ.
Is ssam available in the United States?
Yes. Ssam and perilla have been introduced through Korean barbecue restaurants and food media in the United States.

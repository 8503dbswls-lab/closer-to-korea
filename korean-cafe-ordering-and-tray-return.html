My Korean Café Routine: Seats, Orders, and Returning Cups

How I find a seat, order at the counter, wait for my food, and return the dishes before leaving.

Published 2026-11-10

When I visit a café in Korea, I usually find a seat before going to the counter. The small steps after that are familiar to me, but they may be less obvious on a first visit. Here is how I do it.

First, I find a seat and place my order

I choose a seat, put my things down, and then go to the counter to order what I want to eat or drink. This is my own routine. If a café gives different seating or ordering instructions, follow those, and keep valuables with you when you leave the table.

Where I wait depends on the buzzer

If the staff give me a vibrating buzzer, called a 진동벨 in Korean, I take it back to my seat and wait there. When it alerts me, I go to collect my order.

If I am not given a buzzer, I wait near the counter instead. Once my order is ready, I carry it to my seat. Leave the ordering and pickup space clear while you wait, and follow any instructions from the staff.

I return the dishes before leaving

After that, I spend my time in the café doing whatever I came to do. When I am finished and ready to leave, I take my used cups and dishes with me.

If there is a self-service return station, I put them there. If there is no return station, I take them back to the counter. I do not leave them on the table as part of my usual routine.

A useful question if you are unsure

“이거 어디에 반납하면 되나요?” means “Where should I return this?” You can ask staff this while indicating your cup or tray.

This guide is based on my own café visits. Use it as a starting point, then follow the signs and staff instructions at the café you visit.
